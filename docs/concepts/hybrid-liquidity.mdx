## Two different forms of liquidity

<Columns cols={2}>
<Card title="Active orders" icon="list-ordered">
Explicit limit orders carry price, quantity and sequence. Matching can enumerate fills and assign them to makers.
</Card>
<Card title="Passive liquidity" icon="waves">
A compressed liquidity representation describes a marginal price and liquidity boundaries without representing every unit as a discrete order.
</Card>
</Columns>

Hybrid's engine models both in the quoting layer. It can compare explicit order prices against passive marginal price boundaries and plan execution across price levels.

## The important boundary

<Warning>
The **funded six-stage verification covers collateralized active ask execution**, not general production settlement against passive LP vaults. Some active-only instructions intentionally reject amounts that would require passive liquidity.
</Warning>

## Fixed-point prices

The Rust engine stores prices and square-root prices using **Q64 fixed-point** values. Order quantities and token balances are raw integer atoms, not display-decimal floats.

- `price_x64`: fixed-point order price.
- `sqrt_price_x64`: square-root price representation used by the quoting engine.
- `base_qty`: base token quantity in raw atoms.
- `sequence`: monotonic allocation used for time priority.

Read [Orders and matching](/concepts/orders-and-matching) for what changes at placement and execution.
